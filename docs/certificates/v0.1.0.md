The canonical, evolving provenance record for the `v0.1.0` tag. Created 2026-09-01, and the
reason it exists is structural rather than editorial: the GitHub release body had accumulated
two appended addenda and was about to take a third, which is an addendum ladder — a document
whose corrections are ordered by when they were written rather than by what they correct, each
one readable only after the reader has held the previous two in their head. The release body
now carries the certificate as rendered plus a pointer here. **Corrections accumulate in this
file from now on**, where they are tracked, diffable, reviewable in a pull request, and inside
`citation-lint.sh`'s enumeration on every push.

Three things this file does not do, ever:

- **It does not edit the tag.** The `v0.1.0` tag object and its commit are immutable and
  untouched. Nothing here restates a verdict, revises a tally, or moves a rev.
- **It does not edit history.** A dated record does not go false, it goes *stale*, and
  stale-with-a-date is honest. The release body's own text stays byte-for-byte; what this file
  adds is the reading a person consulting it **today** needs.
- **It does not launder.** Every entry below names what was wrong, when it was found, what
  ruled on it, and which commit fixed it — including where the fix arrived after the tag.

### 1. The certificate log is tracked (2026-08-31)

The log now resolves in-tree at the digest above. **What the digest establishes, and what it
does not:** these notes published no digest when they were written, so there was nothing for a
recovered copy to be checked against. Identity is *corroborated*, not proven, by three witnesses
internal to the artifact — its own rev stamp reads `68a9bec`; its tally matches the published
tally verbatim; its mtime predates the tag by about seventy minutes. Recording the sha256
freezes the bytes from that point forward and cannot show they are the bytes originally cited: a
pin certifies stability, not birth-correctness. `CONTRIBUTING.md`'s tag checklist now requires
both halves at tag time — the certificate log tracked **and** its digest published — because
present and verifiable are different properties and a certificate needs both.

### 2. The judged witness was a lab host (2026-08-31)

`gate-p3`'s throughput sentinel — the criterion that re-checks P2's percent-of-peak floor on the
dispatched microkernel shape — resolved its host set from `.keel-sentinel`, an untracked,
gitignored, machine-local file on the dev host (mtime `2026-08-11`), and that file **outranked
the configured fleet**. In the certificate run P2's floor was judged on exactly one host,
`janus.local`, a lab machine in neither the certificate's fleet nor any judged fleet since the
migration ruling; `keel-skx`, `keel-zen4` and `keel-zen5` each printed *"not a sentinel, so P2's
floor is not judged here"*.

The direction of the finding matters and is stated plainly: the intrusion did not arrive in the
run under suspicion, it was already in the baseline. That is why the `#113` re-measurement's
comparison survives intact — `janus` was the constant on both sides — and why the certificate is
the artifact that needed the correction. A like-for-like comparison stays valid while *both*
sides are off-subject. The affected criterion PASSed on the host it was judged on, and the two
directions matter separately: the reading is off-subject for the fleet, not flattering, and
nothing published divides by that criterion.

Fixed at `a65d39e`: the sentinel set is derived from the fleet and nothing else; `.keel-sentinel`
is not a selection input and its presence is reported as *not read*, with its mtime;
`$KEEL_SENTINEL_HOST` **fails** rather than being silently ignored; an out-of-fleet sentinel is
declared with `$KEEL_SENTINEL_OUT_OF_FLEET`, which unions with the fleet and prints into both the
run's `.cmd` and the log's declaration row. At `afb108e`, `detach.sh` copies every `.keel-*` file
into a run's `.cmd` verbatim, by glob, with mtime, labelled as of launch — because an `unset`
loop cannot reach a file, which is how the record was clean twice by two mechanisms and still
wrong. §5 rule 21 is amended to match: *a total restatement is only as total as the set of
channels it enumerates.*

### 4. The certificate's sentinel reading sat under an unattributed suspicion (2026-09-01)

**What was wrong is what the record did not say.** On 2026-09-01 `gate-p3` at `ba6f286` printed
`sentinel: dispatched 2x32/avx512/kc=128 fell below P2's floor — 43.2% of peak` on `janus.local`,
the same criterion on the same host that this certificate had read at **47.90%** two days earlier.
Two readings 4.7 points apart, and nothing in the tree said which was the outlier — so a reader
could equally conclude the certificate had caught a good moment. That is a certificate resting on
an unadjudicated number, which is a provenance defect whether or not the number turns out to be
right.

Adjudicated by a **null A/B** — one revision, `df999da`, both arms built from it, `n=30` each,
`janus.local`, so the only thing separating the two tables is elapsed time. Re-derived here with
`go run ./tools/benchci` against the tracked logs:

| reading | `Kernel/2x32/avx512/kc=128` | `Peak/avx512` | share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| this certificate, `68a9bec` | 99.52 | 207.75 | **47.90%** |
| `ba6f286`, the FAIL | 89.81 | — | **43.2%** |
| `df999da` arm A | 99.905 ± 0.085% | 208.4 ± 0.048% | **47.94%** |
| `df999da` arm B | 99.815 ± 0.095% | 208.7 ± 0.048% | **47.83%** |

The certificate's 47.90% is the reproducible reading and the 43.2% was a transient excursion. The
within-run interval tells the same story without any cross-run comparison: at `ba6f286` this row's
band was ±1.681% and the neighbouring `4x32` band was ±5.378%, against 0.066–0.147% in both arms
here — an imprecision, not a shift. Which rows widened correlates with touching memory rather than
staying in registers; that is a grouping and not a mechanism, because every arm here is a timing arm
and `#141`'s own checklist rules that *"a timing arm cannot distinguish these"*.

**What this does not do.** It does not retro-certify anything: the tag's gate ran once, on
`68a9bec`, and no later run changes that. The certificate's number is now *corroborated on its own
host* rather than unattributed, and the criterion's exposure to host excursions is written into
`docs/gates.md` under criterion 5b's coverage line — where it belongs, because a green 5b was never
a bound on host stability. That the two arms of one build still differ by −3.08% at `p=0.000` on
`Kernel/2x32/avx512/kc=32` is on `#141` and is not settled by this row.

## Instrument changes after the signing run

**A separate list on purpose, and not a fifth row above.** Nothing in this section says anything was
wrong; each entry says that the *instrument* which produced the pinned log has changed since, so a
reader diffing a re-measurement against `release-a2-68a9bec.log` will find text that no verdict
explains. Filing these as corrections would assert a defect that does not exist, and leaving them out
would make the next raw diff look like a regression — which is the whole reason rule 22 requires a
seam to be disclosed rather than merely survived.

Each entry states the change, whether it moves a **normalized verdict key** (the identity the pinned
log is actually compared by, rule 24), and what a raw text diff will show.

### 1. The builder-toolchain line gains a `flags=[…]` word (2026-09-01)

`remote.sh`'s `builder_toolchain` now reports the build flags stamped **inside** the binary alongside
its toolchain version, read with `go version -m` for exactly the reason the toolchain itself is read
off the artifact and not off the driver's shell (#58). The pinned log holds one such line, at line
1703:

    builder toolchain go1.27.0-X:simd, read off keel.test and not off this shell's go

and a re-measurement renders the same line with the flags after the version — measured at each
stage, not predicted, from `go version -m` on a
`GOEXPERIMENT=simd GOOS=linux GOARCH=amd64 CGO_ENABLED=0 go test -c` build at this revision:

- before `-trimpath` landed: `flags=[-buildmode=exe -compiler=gc]`
- from `-trimpath` onward: `flags=[-buildmode=exe -compiler=gc -trimpath=true]`

Both forms are stated because a reader diffing an *older* re-measurement needs the first one; keying
this entry only to today's wording would leave that reader with an unexplained line.

The extra word is a second word in the same place rather than a second seam — but `-trimpath` also
changes the binary that gets measured, which this entry's last paragraph expressly disclaims of
itself, so that half is **entry 2** and not this one.

**It moves no normalized verdict key.** `info()` prints at eight spaces and the certificate
comparison anchors on `^  (PASS|FAIL|UNMEASURED|BASELINE|REPORTED) ` — two spaces, verdict word — so
this line is outside the key space entirely, in both the before and the after. What a **raw** diff
shows is one changed line out of the log's 1883, and this section is why it is not a finding.

**What it does not do.** It does not change what was measured, which host measured it, or any number
in the log. It changes what the log *says about the binary it measured*, which was previously silent
about flags — and the reason that silence mattered is on #141: two builds of one revision from two
paths are two different binaries, and keel's own between-binary layout floor is 1.71/0.99/1.32%.

### 2. The measured binary is built with `-trimpath` (2026-09-01)

Ruled on #141: *"the null-A/B-builds-two-binaries class dies at the root or it recurs forever; a
project whose experiments are A/Bs cannot have path-dependent builds."* `remote_build_test` now passes
`-trimpath`, so every binary the fleet executes from this revision forward is path-independent.

**This entry exists because it is not entry 1.** Entry 1 changes what the log *says*; this changes
what the log *measured on*. The binary is a different binary: measured on the dev host at this
revision, `5,089,752` bytes without the flag and `5,066,630` with it, a difference of `23,122` bytes,
with `4` distinct digests from `4` build paths collapsing to `1` byte-identical binary. Code placement
moves when a binary shrinks, and keel's between-binary layout floor on the pinned rows is
`1.71 / 0.99 / 1.32%`.

**It moves no normalized verdict key, and it can move the numbers underneath one.** No verdict line's
text changes, so the rule-24 comparison is unaffected. But every absolute `sec/op` and `GFLOP/s` in
`release-a2-68a9bec.log` was measured on the pre-`-trimpath` binary, so a re-measurement may differ on
any timing row by up to the layout floor without anything having regressed. **A percent-of-peak ratio
is the more robust reading across this seam** than either of its terms, because both terms are
measured on the same binary within a run. What a raw diff shows: the flags word from entry 1, plus
timing numbers that were never expected to be bit-reproducible anyway.

**What is not claimed.** That `-trimpath` makes timings *stable* — it makes them
*path-independent*, which is a different and smaller claim. Whether it removes the widened bands
this seam was found chasing is an open measurement on #141, pre-registered there with its own
with/without witness, and it is not settled by this entry.

## How to add a correction here

One row in the table, one section keyed to its own date and subject, and the section says what
was wrong rather than what is now right. **First decide which of the two lists it belongs in**:
something was wrong about the run or its record, and it is a correction; the instrument that
produced the log has since changed and nothing was wrong, and it goes under *Instrument changes*
with its effect on the normalized key space stated. Filing the second as the first asserts a defect
that does not exist, and either way the raw diff a future reader takes must be explained by exactly
one of these lists. Two standing constraints:

- **A criterion is amended only by Scott, and only against a standard that predates the result.**
  Nothing in this file may narrow or widen a criterion; it records what criteria did.
- **Every number here is measured at the moment of writing, from the tracked artifact**, not
  carried from prose. The two digests above were recomputed with `shasum -a 256` against the
  tracked logs on 2026-09-01, and the three shares were recomputed from the archived samples.
